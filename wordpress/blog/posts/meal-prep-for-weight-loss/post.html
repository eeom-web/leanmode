The hardest part of eating well isn't knowing what to eat. It's having something healthy ready when you're hungry, tired and short on time. That's exactly what meal prep solves.

With a couple of hours on the weekend, you can have healthy lunches and dinners ready for most of the week. Here's how to start, even if you've never done it.

Why meal prep helps with weight loss

  • Fewer spontaneous decisions. When a healthy meal is waiting in the fridge, you're far less likely to order takeout.
  • Built-in portion control. You decide the portions once, when you're not hungry.
  • Less stress during the week. No daily "what should I cook?"
  • It saves money. Fewer takeout meals, less food waste.

Meal prep in 5 steps

1. Start small

Don't try to prep every meal for seven days. Start with lunches for four days, or dinners for three. Once that works, add more.

2. Plan with a simple formula

Build every meal with the plate method: half vegetables, a quarter protein, a quarter smart carbs, plus a little healthy fat. Choose:

  • 1 to 2 proteins (for example chicken and eggs, or tofu and lentils)
  • 1 to 2 carbs (for example rice and potatoes)
  • 2 to 3 vegetables (for example broccoli, peppers and salad)
  • 1 sauce or dressing to add flavor

3. Make a shopping list

Write down everything you need, sorted by section of the store. Our weight loss grocery list is a good starting point.

4. Cook in batches

Use your time well and let the oven do most of the work:

  1. Start the rice or potatoes.
  2. Put the protein and vegetables on two baking trays and roast them at 200 °C (400 °F) for 20 to 30 minutes.
  3. Meanwhile, boil eggs and wash and cut salad vegetables.
  4. Mix a simple sauce, for example yogurt with lemon, garlic and herbs.

5. Portion and store

Divide everything into containers. Keep 3 to 4 days' worth in the fridge and freeze the rest. Glass containers are easy to reheat and clean.

A sample meal prep week

You don't have to prep every meal. Many people prep lunches and breakfasts and cook simple, fresh dinners.

Food safety basics

  • Let food cool down for no more than 2 hours before putting it in the fridge.
  • Eat cooked meals from the fridge within 3 to 4 days.
  • Freeze anything you won't eat within that time and thaw it in the fridge.
  • Reheat until hot all the way through.

Tips that make meal prep easier

  • Use the same base, different flavors. Chicken and rice tastes different with salsa, curry sauce or pesto.
  • Prep ingredients, not just meals. Washed salad, cooked grains and roasted vegetables can be combined in different ways.
  • Keep it simple. Recipes with five to seven ingredients are perfect for meal prep.
  • Put on music or a podcast. It makes the two hours fly by.

The bottom line

Meal prep is one of the most practical habits for weight loss. Start with a few lunches, use the plate method for every container and build from there. In a few weeks, it'll feel completely normal.
