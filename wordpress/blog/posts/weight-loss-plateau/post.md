The first weeks go well, the scale goes down, your clothes get looser. And then, suddenly, nothing. Same weight for two, three, four weeks. A plateau can feel very discouraging, but it's a normal part of losing weight. Here's why it happens and what to do.

## Why weight loss plateaus happen

- **Your body needs less energy.** A smaller body uses fewer calories. The amount of food that made you lose weight at the start might now be just enough to maintain.
- **Habits slowly slip.** Portions get a little bigger, snacks come back, the walks get shorter. That's human.
- **Water hides fat loss.** Stress, salty food, hormones and new workouts can make your body hold water and hide fat loss for a while.

## First: are you really stuck?

Your weight can go up and down by 1 to 2 kg (2 to 4 lb) from day to day. Before you change anything:

- Weigh yourself at the same time each day and look at the **weekly average**.
- Measure your **waist** once a week.
- Notice how your **clothes** fit.

If your weekly average hasn't moved for **3 to 4 weeks** and your waist hasn't changed, it's a real plateau.

## 8 ways to break a plateau

### 1. Check your portions again

Over time, portions tend to grow. For one week, measure or write down what you eat. Many people find a few extra hundred calories they weren't aware of.

### 2. Look at liquid calories and extras

Coffee drinks, juice, alcohol, cooking oil, dressings and "just a bite" here and there add up. See our list of [low-calorie drinks](/low-calorie-drinks/).

### 3. Eat more protein

Protein keeps you full and helps you keep your muscle. Include a palm-sized portion at every meal.

### 4. Fill up on vegetables

Make half of your plate vegetables, the core of the [plate method](/plate-method-for-weight-loss/). It lets you eat the same amount of food with fewer calories.

### 5. Move more during the day

Your daily steps matter as much as your workouts. Add 2,000 steps a day with an extra walk. Our [walking plan](/walking-for-weight-loss/) helps.

### 6. Add or increase strength training

Strength training helps you keep muscle and changes your body shape even when the scale is slow. Try our [home workout for beginners](/beginner-home-workout/).

### 7. Sleep and manage stress

Poor sleep and stress make hunger stronger and can cause water retention. See our tips on [sleep and weight loss](/sleep-and-weight-loss/).

### 8. Be patient and consistent

Sometimes the scale stays the same for a few weeks and then suddenly drops. If you're eating well and moving, keep going. Consistency beats perfection.

## What not to do

- **Don't crash diet.** Cutting your food drastically leads to hunger, low energy and usually to overeating later.
- **Don't add hours of exercise.** It's hard to keep up and often makes you hungrier.
- **Don't give up.** A plateau doesn't mean your plan isn't working. It means it's time for a small adjustment.

## Is it time to take a break?

If you've been losing weight for many months, it can help to spend a few weeks eating at a maintenance level: same healthy habits, slightly bigger portions. Then continue. Learning to maintain is a skill you'll need anyway.

> Not losing weight at all for months despite consistent changes? Talk to your doctor to rule out medical reasons. Our article [why am I not losing weight?](/why-am-i-not-losing-weight/) covers other common causes.

## The bottom line

Plateaus are normal. Check whether you're really stuck, look honestly at portions and extras, move a little more and stay consistent. Small adjustments are usually all it takes.
