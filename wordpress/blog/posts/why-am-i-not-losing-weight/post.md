You're eating better, moving more and the scale still won't budge. It's frustrating, and it makes many people give up. But there's almost always a reason, and most of them are easy to fix once you know them.

Here are ten common reasons why you're not losing weight, and what to do about each one.

## 1. You're eating more than you think

Most people underestimate how much they eat, often by a lot. A spoon of peanut butter becomes three, a "handful" of nuts becomes two.

**What to do:** for one week, write down everything you eat and drink, or take a photo of every meal. You don't have to do it forever, just long enough to spot patterns.

## 2. Liquid calories

Juice, soda, smoothies, sweet coffee drinks, alcohol: they add calories but don't fill you up.

**What to do:** switch to water, sparkling water, unsweetened tea or black coffee most of the time. See our guide to [what to drink when you're losing weight](/low-calorie-drinks/).

## 3. "Healthy" foods in big portions

Nuts, olive oil, avocado, granola and dried fruit are healthy, but they're also calorie-dense.

**What to do:** keep the healthy foods, but measure the portion: a small handful of nuts, a thumb of oil, two tablespoons of granola.

## 4. The weekend effect

Five good days and two days of eating out, drinks and snacks can cancel out the whole week.

**What to do:** enjoy your weekend, but plan it. Choose one special meal or treat instead of eating freely for two days. Our [restaurant tips](/eating-out-while-losing-weight/) help too.

## 5. Too little protein

Without enough protein, you get hungry faster and snack more.

**What to do:** include a palm-sized portion of protein at every meal. Start with our [high-protein breakfast ideas](/high-protein-breakfast-ideas/).

## 6. Not enough sleep

Short nights make you hungrier and your cravings stronger.

**What to do:** aim for 7 to 9 hours. Our tips on [sleep and weight loss](/sleep-and-weight-loss/) can help.

## 7. Overestimating exercise

A 30-minute workout might burn 200 to 300 calories. That's easy to eat back with one snack or drink as a "reward".

**What to do:** see exercise as a bonus for your health, not as a license to eat more. Food has a bigger effect on weight loss.

## 8. Weighing yourself the wrong way

Your weight goes up and down by 1 to 2 kg (2 to 4 lb) from day to day because of water, salt, hormones and digestion. One weigh-in says very little.

**What to do:** weigh yourself at the same time (for example in the morning after the bathroom) and look at the weekly average. Also measure your waist.

## 9. You're building muscle

If you've just started strength training, you might lose fat and gain a little muscle at the same time. The scale stays the same, but your clothes fit better.

**What to do:** track more than the scale: waist measurement, photos and how your clothes fit.

## 10. You've hit a plateau

After a few weeks or months of losing weight, it's normal to slow down. Your smaller body needs a bit less energy than before.

**What to do:** read our guide on [how to break a weight loss plateau](/weight-loss-plateau/).

## When to talk to your doctor

Some medical conditions (like an underactive thyroid or PCOS) and some medications can make weight loss harder. If you've made changes for a few months without any progress, or if you're gaining weight without changing anything, talk to your doctor.

## The bottom line

When weight loss stalls, there's usually a simple reason: hidden calories, liquid calories, weekends, too little protein or too little sleep. Pick the one that sounds most like you and work on it for two weeks. Small fixes often make the biggest difference.
