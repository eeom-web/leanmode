Healthy eating starts in the grocery store. If your kitchen is full of good food, healthy meals are easy. If it's full of snacks and ready meals, they'll end up on your plate.

Here's a simple grocery list with 50 staples, sorted by store section. You don't need all of them, pick what you like and build from there.

Protein

  1. Eggs
  2. Chicken or turkey breast
  3. Lean ground beef or turkey
  4. Salmon or other fish (fresh or frozen)
  5. Canned tuna in water
  6. Shrimp (frozen)
  7. Greek yogurt or skyr
  8. Cottage cheese
  9. Tofu or tempeh
  10. Canned beans and lentils

Vegetables

  1. Broccoli
  2. Spinach or other leafy greens
  3. Salad greens
  4. Bell peppers
  5. Tomatoes and cherry tomatoes
  6. Cucumber
  7. Zucchini
  8. Carrots
  9. Onions and garlic
  10. Frozen mixed vegetables

Frozen vegetables are just as healthy as fresh ones, cheap and always ready.

Fruit

  1. Berries (fresh or frozen)
  2. Apples
  3. Bananas
  4. Oranges or clementines
  5. Lemons
  6. Kiwis or seasonal fruit

Smart carbs

  1. Oats
  2. Potatoes and sweet potatoes
  3. Brown or white rice
  4. Quinoa
  5. Whole-grain bread
  6. Whole-wheat pasta
  7. Whole-wheat tortillas

Healthy fats

  1. Olive oil
  2. Avocados
  3. Nuts (almonds, walnuts)
  4. Seeds (chia, flax, pumpkin)
  5. Peanut butter (just peanuts and salt)
  6. Cheese (in small amounts)

Pantry and flavor

  1. Canned tomatoes
  2. Low-sodium broth
  3. Spices and dried herbs
  4. Mustard
  5. Salsa
  6. Hot sauce
  7. Vinegar
  8. Herbal and green tea
  9. Coffee
  10. Sparkling water
  11. Dark chocolate (for a square or two)

Tips for smarter shopping

  • Plan your meals first. Know what you'll cook this week before you go. Our meal prep guide shows you how.
  • Shop with a list and stick to it. Lists help you avoid impulse buys.
  • Don't shop hungry. Everything looks tempting on an empty stomach.
  • Shop the edges first. Fresh foods are usually around the outside of the store, packaged snacks in the middle.
  • Read labels. Check the serving size, sugar and ingredient list, especially for "healthy" products like granola, protein bars and yogurt drinks.
  • Buy frozen. Frozen vegetables, fruit and fish save money and reduce waste.

What to buy less often

You don't have to ban anything. But it's easier when these foods aren't always at home: soda and juice, chips and crackers, cookies and sweets, sugary cereals, ready meals and pastries. Our list of healthy food swaps has easy alternatives.

The bottom line

A well-stocked kitchen makes healthy eating the easy choice. Start with the basics from this list, combine them with the plate method and you'll always have the ingredients for a filling, balanced meal.
