That afternoon moment when you'd do anything for a cookie. The chocolate after dinner that turns into half a bar. Sugar cravings are one of the biggest obstacles when you're trying to lose weight.

The good news: cravings usually have a reason, and you can do a lot to make them weaker.

Why sugar cravings happen

  • Long gaps between meals. When you're very hungry, your body wants fast energy.
  • Meals without protein and fiber. A breakfast of white toast and jam leaves you hungry and craving more sugar soon after.
  • Habit. If you always have something sweet after lunch, your brain starts expecting it.
  • Tiredness and stress. Sugar feels like a quick pick-me-up when you're exhausted or stressed.
  • Strict rules. Completely banning sweets can make you think about them even more.

11 tips to stop sugar cravings

1. Eat protein at every meal

Protein keeps you full and your energy steady. It's one of the most effective ways to reduce cravings. Try our high-protein breakfast ideas.

2. Add fiber

Vegetables, fruit, oats, beans and whole grains slow down digestion. Here's a list of high-fiber foods.

3. Don't skip meals

Regular, balanced meals prevent the extreme hunger that leads to sugar binges.

4. Drink water

Sometimes thirst feels like a craving. Drink a glass of water and wait ten minutes.

5. Sleep enough

Tired people crave more sugar. Aim for 7 to 9 hours. More in our article on sleep and weight loss.

6. Don't keep sweets in sight

Keep sweets out of the house, or at least out of sight. If you have to go to the store to get them, you'll eat them far less often.

7. Wait 10 minutes

Cravings come in waves. Set a timer for 10 minutes and do something else. Often, the craving is gone or much weaker when the timer rings.

8. Break the habit chain

If you always want something sweet at the same time or place, change the routine: go for a short walk after lunch, brush your teeth after dinner or have a cup of tea instead.

9. Choose a better sweet

When you want something sweet, try:

  • fresh berries with Greek yogurt
  • frozen grapes or banana slices
  • an apple with cinnamon and a little peanut butter
  • one or two squares of dark chocolate
  • a cup of hot cocoa made with milk and unsweetened cocoa

10. Allow yourself treats, on purpose

Banning sweets completely often backfires. Plan a treat you really enjoy, eat it slowly and without guilt, and move on.

11. Reduce sugar step by step

Your taste buds adapt. If you take two sugars in your coffee, go to one, then half. After a few weeks, very sweet foods start to taste too sweet.

How much sugar is okay?

Health organizations recommend keeping added sugar low: for example, the World Health Organization recommends less than 10% of your daily calories from free sugars, ideally less than 5%. That's about 25 to 50 grams a day for most adults. The natural sugar in whole fruit and plain milk isn't the problem.

The bottom line

Sugar cravings are normal, and they're not a sign of weak willpower. Eat regular meals with protein and fiber, sleep enough and change the habits around your cravings. And when you do have something sweet, enjoy it.
