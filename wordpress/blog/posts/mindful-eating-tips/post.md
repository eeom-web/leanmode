How often do you eat in front of a screen, finish your plate without noticing and then wonder where the food went? Most of us eat on autopilot at least some of the time.

Mindful eating is the opposite: paying attention while you eat. It sounds simple, and it is. But it can help you eat less, enjoy your food more and stop overeating without following a strict diet.

## What is mindful eating?

Mindful eating means eating with your full attention. You notice what you eat, how it tastes, how hungry you are and when you start to feel full. There are no forbidden foods and no calorie counting.

## Why it helps with weight loss

- **You notice when you're full.** It takes about 20 minutes for your brain to register fullness. Eating slowly gives it time to catch up.
- **You eat less without trying.** Many people naturally eat smaller portions when they pay attention.
- **You enjoy food more.** When you really taste your food, a smaller portion can be just as satisfying.
- **You separate hunger from other feelings.** Mindful eating helps you notice when you're eating because of boredom or stress, not hunger.

## 9 simple mindful eating habits

### 1. Check your hunger before you eat

Rate your hunger from 1 (starving) to 10 (stuffed). Ideally, start eating at around 3 to 4 and stop at around 6 to 7: satisfied, not full.

### 2. Sit down at a table

Eating standing in the kitchen, in the car or on the couch makes it easy to eat more than you planned. Sit down, even for a snack.

### 3. Turn off screens

TV, phone and laptop distract you from your food and your fullness signals. Try at least one screen-free meal a day.

### 4. Use a smaller plate

A full small plate feels more satisfying than a half-empty large one. It's a simple trick that works.

### 5. Put your fork down between bites

This automatically slows you down. Chew properly before you take the next bite.

### 6. Take a pause halfway through

Stop for a minute in the middle of your meal. Drink some water and check: am I still hungry?

### 7. Notice taste and texture

Pay attention to the first few bites. What does it taste like? Is it crunchy, creamy, warm? It sounds strange at first, but it makes meals more enjoyable.

### 8. Stop when you're satisfied

You don't have to finish your plate. Save leftovers for later. Being "satisfied" is the goal, not being "full".

### 9. Ask yourself why you want to eat

Before you reach for a snack, ask: am I hungry, or am I bored, tired, stressed or sad? If it's not hunger, food won't fix it. Read more in our article on [emotional eating](/emotional-eating/).

## Mindful eating and portion size

Mindful eating works even better together with a simple structure for your meals. The [plate method](/plate-method-for-weight-loss/) gives you balanced portions, and mindful eating helps you notice when you've had enough.

## Start small

Don't try all nine habits at once. Pick one meal a day, for example dinner, and try two habits: sit down without screens and put your fork down between bites. After a week, add another.

## The bottom line

Mindful eating isn't a diet, it's a way of eating. Slow down, pay attention and stop when you're satisfied. It's one of the simplest ways to eat less without feeling like you're missing out.
