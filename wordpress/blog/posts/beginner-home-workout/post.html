When you want to lose weight, it's tempting to focus only on cardio. But strength training is just as important: it helps you keep your muscles while you lose fat, makes everyday life easier and shapes your body.

You don't need a gym. This 20-minute workout uses only your body weight and works for complete beginners.

Why strength training helps with weight loss

  • You keep your muscle. When you eat less, your body can lose muscle along with fat. Strength training signals that the muscle is still needed.
  • You look and feel firmer. Losing fat while keeping muscle changes your shape more than the number on the scale shows.
  • Everyday life gets easier. Carrying groceries, climbing stairs and getting up from the floor all become easier.

How the workout works

  • Warm up for 3 minutes: march on the spot, circle your arms and shoulders, do a few gentle squats.
  • Do each exercise for 40 seconds, then rest for 20 seconds.
  • After all 6 exercises, rest for 1 to 2 minutes.
  • Repeat 3 rounds. That's about 20 minutes including the warm-up.

Do this workout 2 to 3 times a week with at least one rest day in between.

The 6 exercises

1. Squats

Stand with your feet shoulder-width apart. Push your hips back as if you're sitting down on a chair, keep your chest up, then stand up again. Easier: sit down on a real chair and stand up.

2. Wall or knee push-ups

Place your hands on a wall (easiest), on a table or on the floor with your knees down. Lower your chest towards your hands and push back. Keep your body in a straight line.

3. Glute bridges

Lie on your back with your knees bent and feet flat on the floor. Squeeze your glutes and lift your hips until your body forms a straight line from knees to shoulders. Lower slowly.

4. Reverse lunges

Step back with one leg and lower your back knee towards the floor. Push back up and switch legs. Easier: hold on to a chair or wall for balance.

5. Plank

Rest on your forearms and toes, or on your knees for an easier version. Keep your body straight and your stomach tight. Hold for as long as you can within the 40 seconds.

6. Bird dog

Start on your hands and knees. Stretch your right arm forward and your left leg back, hold for a second, then switch sides. Great for your back and core.

How to progress

When an exercise feels easy, make it harder: slow down, pause at the bottom of each movement or choose the next harder version.

Tips for beginners

  • Quality before speed. Slow, controlled movements are better than fast, sloppy ones.
  • Breathe. Don't hold your breath, breathe out during the hard part of each movement.
  • Pain is a stop sign. Muscle burn is normal, sharp pain is not.
  • Combine it with walking. Two to three strength workouts plus daily walks is a great routine. Try our 4-week walking plan.
  • Eat enough protein. It helps your muscles recover. See our high-protein breakfast ideas.

If you have joint problems, back pain, heart problems or another health condition, ask your doctor which exercises are right for you.

The bottom line

Twenty minutes, two to three times a week, is enough to start. Keep it simple, focus on good form and make it a little harder every couple of weeks. In a month, you'll be surprised how much stronger you feel.
