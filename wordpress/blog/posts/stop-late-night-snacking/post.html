You eat well all day, and then the evening comes. You sit down on the couch, and suddenly you're in the kitchen looking for something. Chips, chocolate, a bowl of cereal. Sound familiar?

Late-night snacking is one of the most common reasons weight loss stalls. The good news: it's usually a habit, not a lack of willpower, and habits can be changed.

Why we snack at night

  • You didn't eat enough during the day. Skipping breakfast or eating a tiny lunch often backfires in the evening.
  • Boredom and habit. TV plus couch plus snacks is a strong routine.
  • Stress and tiredness. After a long day, food feels like a reward or a way to relax.
  • Lack of sleep. Being tired makes cravings stronger, especially for sweet and salty foods.
  • Easy access. If the cookies are in the cupboard, you'll think about them.

10 tips to stop late-night snacking

1. Eat enough during the day

Have a proper breakfast with protein, a filling lunch and a balanced dinner. If you go into the evening really hungry, no tip in the world will help. Try one of our high-protein breakfast ideas.

2. Make dinner filling

Build your dinner with the plate method: half vegetables, a quarter protein, a quarter carbs. Protein and fiber keep you full for longer.

3. Set a "kitchen closed" time

Decide on a time, for example 8 p.m. or one hour after dinner, after which the kitchen is closed. Many people find a clear rule easier than deciding each evening.

4. Brush your teeth after dinner

It sounds simple, but it's a strong signal that eating is over for the day. And food doesn't taste great with fresh toothpaste.

5. Don't keep trigger foods at home

If you can't stop at one cookie, don't buy them. Out of sight really is out of mind.

6. Find a new evening routine

Replace "TV and snacks" with something else for your hands and mind: a short walk, a shower, a book, a puzzle, stretching or a hobby.

7. Drink something warm

A cup of herbal tea, decaf tea or warm milk can feel like a treat and helps you wind down.

8. Ask yourself: am I really hungry?

Before you open the fridge, pause and ask: would I eat an apple right now? If not, you're probably not hungry, you're bored, tired or stressed. Our article on emotional eating has more on this.

9. Plan a small evening snack if you need one

If you're truly hungry later in the evening, plan a small, filling snack instead of fighting it: Greek yogurt, a piece of fruit or a few nuts. Put it in a bowl and eat it at the table, not in front of the TV.

10. Go to bed earlier

The longer you're awake, the more time there is to snack, and tiredness makes cravings worse. Read our tips on sleep and weight loss.

What if you slip up?

It happens. One evening of snacking doesn't ruin anything. Don't punish yourself by skipping breakfast the next day, that only starts the cycle again. Just get back to your normal routine with the next meal.

The bottom line

Late-night snacking is mostly a habit that grows from being too hungry, too tired or too bored. Eat enough during the day, set a clear end to eating and build a new evening routine. Pick two tips from this list and try them for one week.
