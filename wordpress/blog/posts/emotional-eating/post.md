Almost everyone eats for emotional reasons sometimes: ice cream after a bad day, chips when you're bored, chocolate when you're stressed. Food is comforting, and that's okay once in a while.

But when eating becomes the main way to deal with feelings, it can make weight loss very hard and leave you feeling guilty. Here's how to recognize emotional eating and what to do instead.

## Physical hunger vs. emotional hunger

| Physical hunger | Emotional hunger |
|---|---|
| Comes on slowly | Comes on suddenly |
| Any food will do | You crave something specific, often sweet or salty |
| You stop when you're full | You keep eating even when you're full |
| You feel satisfied afterwards | You often feel guilty afterwards |
| Starts in your stomach | Starts in your head |

## Common triggers

- **Stress:** work, family, money worries
- **Boredom:** evenings on the couch, long afternoons
- **Tiredness:** after a long day or a bad night
- **Loneliness or sadness**
- **Reward:** "I've earned this"
- **Habit:** always eating while watching TV

Knowing your own triggers is the first step.

## What to do instead

### 1. Pause and name the feeling

Before you eat, stop for a moment and ask: am I physically hungry? If not, what am I feeling right now? Simply naming the feeling ("I'm stressed", "I'm bored") often makes the urge weaker.

### 2. Use the 10-minute rule

Wait ten minutes before you eat. Drink a glass of water, step outside or do something else. If you're still hungry after ten minutes, eat something, mindfully.

### 3. Have a list of alternatives ready

Write down a few things that help you feel better without food, and keep the list on your phone:

- go for a 10-minute walk
- call or text a friend
- take a shower or bath
- listen to music or a podcast
- stretch or do a few minutes of breathing exercises
- write down what's on your mind
- tidy up one small area

### 4. Deal with the cause

If stress is the trigger, what can you change? If boredom is the trigger, what could fill your evenings? Food solves the feeling for a few minutes, the cause is still there afterwards.

### 5. Don't keep trigger foods within reach

It's much easier to resist chips in the store than chips in your cupboard.

### 6. Eat regular, balanced meals

When you're very hungry, emotions and hunger mix and everything feels urgent. Regular meals with protein and fiber make cravings weaker.

### 7. Practice mindful eating

Eat slowly, without screens, and really taste your food. Our guide to [mindful eating](/mindful-eating-tips/) shows you how.

### 8. Be kind to yourself

Emotional eating isn't a failure of willpower. If it happens, don't punish yourself by skipping meals. Just go back to your normal routine with the next meal.

## When to get support

If you often eat large amounts of food in a short time and feel out of control, or if food and weight take up a lot of your thoughts, talk to your doctor or a therapist. Binge eating and other eating disorders are common and can be treated. You don't have to deal with it alone.

## The bottom line

Emotional eating is a habit that develops for understandable reasons. Learn to tell physical from emotional hunger, find your triggers and build a few new ways to cope. It takes practice, so be patient with yourself.
